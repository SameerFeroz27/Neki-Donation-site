Website blueprint

Neki

Donation website — build plan

A single-page donation site: the app name on top, what the app does below it, then the cases you can donate to. Each case opens a payment sheet with two ways to pay — Request to Pay through Raast, or credit / debit card. This document is the blueprint: screens, flows, design system, data, payments and the build phases.

1Overview & goals

Neki is a donation website for verified cases. A visitor lands on one page, reads what the app does, and scrolls through the cases they can give to. A Donate Now button on any case opens a payment sheet, and the sheet offers exactly two ways to pay: Request to Pay (Raast) and credit / debit card.

The brief, restated

App name on top → details of the app below it → the cases you can donate to → a Donate Now button under each case → a payment sheet that opens → two transaction methods: Request to Pay and Credit / Debit Card.

What the product has to achieve

Trust

  • Every case is verified before it is listed
  • Progress, goal and days left shown on the card itself
  • Funds released in stages against bills and photos

Speed

  • No account needed to donate
  • Amount → method → done in three screens
  • Request to Pay works from the bank app the donor already has

Reach

  • Two payment rails so nobody is turned away
  • Raast for local donors, card for overseas and larger gifts
  • Monthly giving for repeat donors

Audience

PrimaryIndividual donors in Pakistan, giving PKR 500 – 50,000, often on a phone SecondaryOverseas Pakistanis giving by card, and repeat monthly donors Not in phase 1Corporate giving, zakat calculation tools, volunteer sign-up, public donor profiles

Success measures

MeasureTargetWhy it matters
Donation sheet completion> 70% of opened sheetsThe two-method design exists to remove drop-off
Mobile share of donations> 75%Drives the mobile-first layout decision
Case funding rate> 80% of listed cases reach goalNothing damages trust like a case that stalls
Repeat donors> 25% give a second timeMonthly giving and receipts are built for this
Scope note. The frontend prototype for all six screens is already built and click-tested. This blueprint describes that design plus the work required to turn it into a live product.

3User flows

Flow A — Request to Pay (Raast)

The donor never types card details. The money moves inside their own bank app, approved with their PIN.

#Donor seesSystem does
1Home page with casesServes the case list, cached for a minute
2Taps Donate Now on a caseOpens the sheet on the amount step with that case attached
3Picks an amount and one-time or monthlyRecomputes the total, including the optional 2% fee cover
4Chooses Request to PayCreates a pending donation with a unique reference
5Switches Mobile ID / IBAN and enters the IDValidates the format locally, then on the server
6Taps Send requestAsks the payment provider to raise a Raast request against that ID
7Notification in their bank appWaits for the provider webhook; shows "waiting for approval"
8Approves with their PINWebhook arrives: marks the donation paid, allocates it to the case
9Receipt with a receipt ID, and an emailIssues the receipt, emails it, updates the case progress
Expiry. A request that is not approved within five minutes is marked expired. The donor can resend it, and no money has moved. Expired requests must never appear as failed donations.

Flow B — Credit / Debit card

#Donor seesSystem does
1–3Same as Flow A, up to choosing the amountSame
4Chooses Credit / Debit CardLoads the card checkout with the amount and case shown
5Optionally switches the display currency to USDConverts for display only; the charge stays in PKR
6Enters email, card number, expiry, CVC, name, countryCard fields are the provider's hosted fields — our server never sees the number
7Taps DonateProvider tokenises the card and may show a 3-D Secure challenge
8Bank challenge screen, if requiredWaits for the authorisation result
9Receipt, plus an emailed copyRecords the payment, issues the receipt, updates progress
Declines. A declined card keeps the donor on the card step with the reason shown, and the entered details intact except the CVC. Never send them back to the start.

6Design system

Two colours only — deep green and gold on an ivory paper background. Green carries trust and the money side; gold carries generosity and draws the eye to progress and calls to action. Two Islamic details are used sparingly: a khatam eight-point star tiled behind the page at very low opacity, and a mihrab arch on each case card.

Accessibility

  • Body text on ivory is at least 7:1 contrast; the muted grey is checked against every background it sits on
  • The sheet traps focus while open, closes on Escape and on a backdrop click, and returns focus to the button that opened it
  • Every field has a real label; errors are text, not colour alone, and are announced
  • Tap targets are at least 44px; the sheet is fully usable at 360px wide
  • Progress bars carry the numbers as text, so nothing depends on reading a bar

7Data model

Four entities carry the whole donation flow. Amounts are stored as integers in the smallest unit to avoid rounding errors.

Case

{ id: "heart-zainab", title: "Heart surgery for Zainab, 6", category: "Health", // Health · Food · Water · Education · Emergency · Seasonal summary: "A hole in the heart needs urgent repair…", story: "…full text for the detail page…", goalAmount: 85000000, // PKR 850,000 in paisa raisedAmount:62000000, currency: "PKR", deadline: "2026-10-17", verifiedAt: "2026-09-28", status: "active", // draft · active · funded · closed media: [ { type:"photo", url:"…", caption:"…" } ], payouts: [ { amount:…, releasedAt:…, receipts:[…] } ] }

Donation

Payment attempt and receipt

PaymentAttempt { id, donationId, provider: "psp_name", providerRef: "…", // the provider's own id, for reconciliation rail: "raast" | "card", raastId: "03XXXXXXXXX", // Mobile ID or IBAN, never stored in full for cards cardLast4: "4242", // only ever the last four cardBrand: "visa", status, errorCode, rawWebhook[] } Receipt { id: "NK-7F2QA9KD", donationId, caseId, amount, currency, method, issuedAt, pdfUrl }
Never stored. Full card numbers, CVCs, Raast PINs or bank credentials. Card data lives only inside the payment provider; we keep the last four digits and the brand so the receipt can say "Visa ···· 4242".

Case progress

raisedAmount is derived from paid donations, not written by hand, so the progress bar can never disagree with the money received. A nightly job reconciles our totals against the provider's settlement report and flags any gap.

8Payments blueprint

Rail 1 — Request to Pay over Raast

StepDetail
Donor enters IDRaast Mobile ID (11 digits, 03XXXXXXXXX) or an IBAN (24 characters, starting PK). Format is checked in the browser, then again on the server.
We create a donationStatus pending, with a unique reference and the amount locked in.
We ask the providerServer-to-server call to raise a request-to-pay against that Raast ID. The provider holds the relationship with the bank.
Donor approvesA notification appears in their bank app; they approve with their PIN. No PIN or credential ever reaches us.
Webhook confirmsThe provider calls our webhook. Signature is verified, the event is de-duplicated by its id, and the donation moves to paid.
Receipt and progressReceipt issued and emailed; the case progress updates from the paid total.
ExpiryNo approval within five minutes → expired. The donor may resend. Expired is not a failure.

Rail 2 — Cards

StepDetail
Hosted fieldsThe card number, expiry and CVC are entered into fields rendered by the provider inside iframes. They never touch our DOM or our server.
TokeniseThe provider returns a token; we send the token, the amount and the donor's email to create the charge.
3-D SecureThe bank may challenge the donor. We show the provider's challenge and wait for the result.
Resultpaid, or failed with a decline reason shown on the card step with the details preserved.
Saved cardsOnly the provider's token is stored against the donor, never a number.

Rules that apply to both rails

  • Server-side amounts. The amount is recalculated on the server from the donation record; the browser's figure is never trusted.
  • Idempotency. Every create-payment call carries a key, so a double tap or a retry cannot charge twice.
  • Webhooks are the truth. A donation only becomes paid from a verified webhook, never from a browser redirect.
  • Out-of-order events. Events are stored raw and applied by state machine, so a late event cannot move a paid donation backwards.
  • Refunds. Available for 24 hours if a case closes early, and for a duplicate charge at any time.
  • Reconciliation. A daily job compares our paid totals against the provider settlement report and alerts on any mismatch.
  • PCI scope. With hosted fields and tokens, the site stays out of card-data scope. This must stay true as features are added — it is the single easiest thing to break.
Provider choice. The Raast rail needs a provider that supports request-to-pay and settles to a local account. Which provider we use affects fees, settlement time and the exact webhook contract, so this is a decision for Arif bhai — see section 12.

EmailRequired for card payments; optional for RaastEnter a valid email for your receipt

Behaviour

  • Errors appear under the field that caused them, as text with an icon or border change, never colour alone
  • The form is validation-neutral while typing and validates on submit; the card number and expiry reformat as you type
  • Server-side validation repeats every rule above — the browser check is for speed, not security
  • Rate limiting: five payment attempts per Raast ID and per IP per ten minutes

What phase 1 already settles

  • The exact wording and hierarchy of the home page, so copy can be written against it
  • The three-step donation flow and both payment paths, so the provider integration has a fixed contract to implement
  • The complete visual system, so case photography and documents can be commissioned to fit the arch format
  • Every validation rule and error message, so backend and frontend agree before either is built

12Assumptions & decisions needed

These are the points where I have made a reasonable assumption to keep moving. Each one changes the build, so they are worth deciding early.

DecisionAssumption madeWhy it matters
Product name "Neki" as a placeholder Appears in the header, hero and footer. Changing it later is quick, but a domain and social handles should be confirmed first.
Currency and payment rails PKR with Raast, matching the reference screens If donors are mainly overseas, card needs to be the primary method, and the USD display becomes a real conversion rather than display-only.
Payment provider Not yet chosen Sets the fee, the settlement time, and the exact webhook contract. Longest lead item — apply first.
Platform fee 0% to the case; the donor may optionally cover 2% processing If a fee is charged, the wording on the card and the receipt has to change, and donors must see it before paying.
Zakat eligibility Tagged per case, shown as a badge Zakat has rules about who may receive and when funds must be disbursed. Cases need to be reviewed against them, and the wording needs a scholar's sign-off.
Verification process Field team verifies each case before listing Who verifies, what evidence is kept, and who can close a case early all need to be defined before the admin panel is built.
Refund policy 24-hour refund if a case closes early Must be published, and it constrains when funds can be released to a case.
Receipts and tax Simple emailed receipt If donors need an official donation receipt for tax, that changes the receipt design and the record keeping.
Monthly giving Offered from day one For Raast this needs a recurring mandate, which not every provider supports. May need to launch card-only and add Raast later.

Recommended next step

  1. Confirm the product name, currency and payment provider so the integration can start.
  2. Approve the six screens as they stand, or mark changes to copy and layout.
  3. Decide the platform fee and the zakat policy, since both change what the donor reads before paying.
  4. Start the payment provider application, then begin phase 2 and phase 3 in parallel.
